## Program Overview

This repository documents the complete set of M&A advisory deliverables produced during the **J.P. Morgan Investment Banking Virtual Experience Program** hosted on Forage. The simulated engagement places the participant in the role of an Analyst on J.P. Morgan's M&A team, advising **WorldWide Brewing Co.** (client) on the potential acquisition of a Hong Kong–based beverage company.

The program spans four sequential tasks that mirror the real-world investment banking deal lifecycle:

| # | Task | Core Activity |
|---|------|---------------|
| 1 | Find M&A Targets | Target screening & preliminary recommendation |
| 2 | Target Information & Auction Process | Company overview & structured auction summary |
| 3 | Financial Analysis | DCF modeling & valuation impact assessment |
| 4 | Investment Recommendation | Revised valuation & buy/hold/sell recommendation |

By the end of the four tasks, the participant has produced a full deal-team deliverable package—from initial target identification through to a final investment recommendation memo.

## Task 1 – Find M&A Targets

### Background

WorldWide Brewing Co. (led by CEO Carlos) is actively pursuing inorganic growth opportunities in the Asia-Pacific beverage sector. The J.P. Morgan Hong Kong Director held a preliminary call with five potential acquisition targets to gauge interest and assess strategic fit. As the deal Analyst, the task was to review the call notes and full transcript, evaluate each target across a defined set of criteria, and draft a concise email recommendation to Carlos.

### Approach & Methodology

The five targets were assessed across four dimensions:
1. **Strategic fit** – alignment with WorldWide Brewing's core beer and spirits portfolio
2. **Ownership structure** – feasibility of a clean, negotiable transaction
3. **Financial health** – revenue trajectory, profitability, and operational stability
4. **Deal complexity** – number of counterparties, regulatory considerations, and public vs. private status

Each target was classified as either **Recommended** or **Not Recommended**, with supporting rationale communicated in a structured email to Carlos.

### Results

| Target | Decision | Key Rationale |
|--------|----------|---------------|
| **HappyHour Co.** | ✅ Recommended | Strong brand, integrated operations, majority stake available via Happy Family holding |
| **Spirit Bay** | ✅ Recommended | Compelling growth trajectory, manageable ownership structure |
| **Bevy's Direct** | ✅ Recommended | Vertically integrated, attractive margins, ownership transfer feasible |
| **Hipsters' Ale** | ❌ Not Recommended | 30 independent shareholders create prohibitive deal complexity and execution risk |
| **Brew Co.** | ❌ Not Recommended | Declining operations, public company process significantly increases timeline and cost |

### Deliverables

| `task1/Task1_Email_Recommendations.docx` | **Final output** – recommendation email to Carlos |

### Conclusion

Of the five targets reviewed, three were recommended for further diligence based on strategic alignment, clean ownership structures, and operational health. HappyHour Co. emerged as the primary target given the availability of a controlling stake through the Happy Family holding company, making it both strategically and structurally the most attractive candidate for a near-term transaction.

## Task 2 – Target Information & Auction Process

### Background

Following Carlos's approval to proceed with HappyHour Co. as the primary target, the deal team required a concise executive briefing document. The task was to synthesise detailed company information—ownership breakdown, historical and projected financials, preliminary valuation ranges, and auction process mechanics—into a clean, one-slide "2-pager" presentation suitable for a CEO audience.

### Approach & Methodology

The 2-pager was structured across two components:

**Company Overview Section**
- Ownership map: Happy Family holds ~60% controlling stake; remainder publicly traded
- Historical financials: Revenue, EBITDA, and margin trends (FY17–FY20E)
- Preliminary valuation using comparable company EV/EBITDA multiples (10.0×–11.5×)

**Auction Process Section**
- Reviewed the formal Process Letter to extract key milestones and bid requirements
- Summarised the two-stage auction timeline with indicative and final bid deadlines
- Outlined documentation requirements (NDA, management presentations, data room access)

## Task 3 – Financial Analysis (DCF Modeling)

### Background

Following the submission of an indicative bid, news emerged that a significant fire at one of HappyHour Co.'s production facilities would materially impact FY21 revenues and profitability. Anna (J.P. Morgan Director) tasked the Analyst with updating the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) model to reflect revised FY21 forecasts and quantify the change in enterprise value, equity value, share price, and acquisition premium.

### Approach & Methodology

**Model Architecture (Perpetuity Growth DCF)**
- **Forecast period:** FY21–FY25 (5-year explicit forecast)
- **Terminal value method:** Gordon Growth Model (perpetuity growth)
- **Discount rate:** WACC (provided as model input)
- **Terminal growth rate (g):** 1.0% (long-run nominal)
- **Valuation date:** FY21 start (discounted to present)

**FY21 Revision Process**
1. Replaced preliminary FY21 revenue ($1,256mm) with fire-adjusted estimate ($1,100mm)
2. Reduced gross margin assumption to reflect lost production leverage
3. Maintained FY22–FY25 growth rates intact (fire treated as one-time, not structural)
4. Recalculated Free Cash Flow (FCF), Net Present Value (NPV), terminal value, and equity bridge

### Results

**FY21 Revised vs. Preliminary Forecast**

| Line Item | Preliminary | Post-Fire | Change |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|--------|
| Revenue | $1,256mm | $1,100mm | −$156mm (−12.4%) |
| Gross Margin % | ~50.5% | 50.0% | −50bps |
| Gross Profit | ~$634mm | $550mm | −$84mm |
| EBITDA | ~$75mm | −$9.8mm | −$84.8mm |

**Valuation Bridge: Preliminary → Revised**

| Metric | Preliminary | Revised (Post-Fire) | Change |
|--------|-------------|---------------------|--------|
| Enterprise Value | $803mm | $738.5mm | −$64.5mm (−8%) |
| Net Debt (assumed) | $85mm | $85mm | — |
| Equity Value | $718mm | $653.9mm | −$64.1mm |
| Share Price | 361c | 328.6c | −32.4c |
| Premium to Market | 118.8% | 99.1% | −19.7pp |

> **Note:** Terminal value represents ~49% of total NPV, meaning the majority of HappyHour's intrinsic value is anchored in FY22+ recovery and long-run growth—largely unaffected by the fire.

### Conclusion

The fire incident caused a meaningful short-term disruption to HappyHour Co.'s FY21 earnings profile, driving EBITDA negative for the year. However, because the incident is non-structural and the company's medium-to-long-term growth trajectory remains intact, the valuation impact was contained to approximately 8% (−$64.5mm on EV). The revised enterprise value of $738.5mm remains comfortably above the competitive bid floor established by DownUnder Beverages ($650mm+), preserving the investment thesis.

## Task 4 – Investment Recommendation

### Background

With the revised DCF model in hand and new intelligence that DownUnder Beverages (a competing bidder) has submitted a binding offer above $650mm, the deal team must decide how WorldWide Brewing should respond. Anna asked the Analyst to prepare a structured recommendation email to the WorldWide Brewing leadership team, synthesising the fire impact, competitive landscape, and revised valuation into a clear, actionable guidance.

### Approach & Methodology

The recommendation was developed across three analytical pillars:

**1. Materiality Assessment of Fire Impact**

| Horizon | Assessment | Rationale |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Short-term (FY21) | **Significant** | EBITDA swings from +$75mm to −$9.8mm; cash burn year |
| Medium-term (FY22–FY25) | **Moderate** | Recovery path to $112mm+ EBITDA; growth rates preserved |
| Long-term (Terminal) | **Limited** | Terminal value (49% of NPV) anchored in structural growth; only 8% total EV impact |

**2. Offsetting Factors**
- The fire is a **one-time operational disruption**, not a structural impairment of the brand, distribution network, or customer relationships
- FY21 NWC release partially offsets the negative EBITDA impact on free cash flow
- Long-run terminal growth rate of 1% remains supportable given category dynamics
- Recovery to positive EBITDA from FY22 onwards ($112mm+) is credible and already forecasted

**3. Competitive Landscape & Bid Credibility**
- DownUnder Beverages submitted a binding offer exceeding $650mm, establishing a credible valuation floor
- Two private equity firms and one US-based brewer also participated in the process, validating the auction's integrity
- The binding offer deadline (Friday) and projected Q3 close indicate sophisticated, well-informed bidders—further supporting the view that the fire impact is not perceived as a deal-breaker by the market

### Results

**Final Valuation Summary**

| Metric | Preliminary | Revised | Change |
|--------|-------------|---------|--------|
| Enterprise Value | $803mm | $738.5mm | −$64.5mm (−8%) |
| Share Price | 361c | 328.6c | −32.4c |
| Acquisition Premium | 118.8% | 99.1% | −19.7pp |
| vs. DownUnder Floor ($650mm) | +23.5% | +13.6% | — |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **binding offer at $738.5mm EV (328.6c/share, ~99% premium)**. This price:
- Remains well above the credible competitive floor of $650mm+ (DownUnder)
- Reflects the actual economic impact of the fire without overpaying pre-incident
- Positions WorldWide Brewing competitively against PE and strategic bidders
- Leaves room to negotiate if DownUnder escalates, given the 13.6% spread to floor

### Conclusion

Despite the short-term earnings shock caused by the fire, HappyHour Co. represents a structurally sound acquisition target with a clear recovery pathway. The revised valuation of $738.5mm reflects a transparent, model-driven adjustment to the preliminary offer, and the active competitive process confirms that the wider market shares this view. The recommendation to proceed at the revised price balances risk management with deal competitiveness.

## Final Conclusion

Throughout this virtual experience program, a complete M&A advisory workflow was executed for WorldWide Brewing Co.'s potential acquisition of HappyHour Co. — from initial target screening to a final, board-ready investment recommendation.

### Investment Case Summary

HappyHour Co. is a well-established Hong Kong beverage company with a strong brand, ~28% EBITDA margins, and a controlling stake available through the Happy Family holding entity. While a warehouse fire created a material FY21 earnings disruption, a rigorous DCF analysis demonstrates that the long-term value drivers remain intact, with the total valuation impact limited to approximately 8%.

### Key Findings

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Revised Enterprise Value** | $738.5mm (down from $803mm preliminary) |
| **Fire Impact** | −8% on EV; contained to FY21; structural growth unaffected |
| **Acquisition Premium** | 99.1% to last traded price (revised from 118.8%) |
| **Competitive Floor** | $650mm+ (DownUnder Beverages binding offer) |
| **Headroom Above Floor** | +13.6% at revised bid price |
| **Terminal Value Weight** | ~49% of NPV — largely insulated from FY21 shock |

### Final Recommendation

> **Proceed with due diligence and submit a competitive binding offer at $738.5mm EV (328.6c/share, ~99% premium to market).**

This recommendation is grounded in three conclusions:
1. The fire is a **transient event**, not a structural impairment of the business
2. The revised valuation remains **13.6% above the credible competitive floor**, providing adequate protection
3. The active participation of DownUnder, two PE firms, and a US brewer **validates the deal at this price level**, and the risk of losing the asset to a competitor outweighs the modest valuation uncertainty
